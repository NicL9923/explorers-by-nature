# Pinewatch terrain and river polish

The northern mountains now have pointed summits, branching ridges and sheltered gullies. A second range beyond the playable valley gives the overlook a longer view. Snow follows elevation with irregular boundaries, while lighter granite makes exposed faces easier to read.

Ground cover mixes green meadow and dry grass at different scales. Grass height, bend, color and density vary across patches. Pine and aspen groves flank the trail with mature canopy trees and smaller saplings. Placement has a 360-tree cap and keeps trunks at least 3.8 metres apart. The last stretch of trail opens into a wider view corridor. Shorelines have combined pebble bars and scattered driftwood alongside the existing reeds.

The river mesh samples ground depth across its width. Shallow water shows a mottled bed color, deeper channels retain their green-blue tint, and small moving highlights break up the surface. The water also receives tree shadows. This is an opaque water shader with an illustrated riverbed, not transparent water or a fluid simulation.

## Ground compatibility

`ValleyShape.Height` preserves the original formula through world z = 210. The ranch, existing river outing and overlook at z = 135 keep their ground heights. Northern mountain heights change and require the server terrain table to be regenerated with the same project state before packaging.

## Assets and cost

All new geometry and textures are generated by project code. Pebble bars reuse the project's original Blender river stones. Each of the 26 bars combines its stones into one renderer and culls at distance. Driftwood uses 48 simple meshes in total. The river has 4,800 triangles. Grass and tree detail still follow the existing quality controls, and scenery does not change shared ranch state.

Validation and actual rendered captures belong in the release's [validation ledger](first-milestone.md). This document records the implementation; it does not claim an unrun performance result.
