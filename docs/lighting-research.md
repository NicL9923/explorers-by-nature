# Lighting research and implementation

Research checked September 13, 2026 against Unity 6.3 LTS and the installed URP 17.3 source. The decision is to repair the game's lighting and material models before migrating pipelines. The linked documentation establishes pipeline capabilities; the captured player views and motion recordings document the visual tradeoffs.

Unity's [current pipeline comparison](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.3/Documentation/Manual/render-pipelines-feature-comparison.html) lists HDRP's built-in volumetric and screen-space GI capabilities. URP does not acquire those by increasing a quality slider. A migration would require replacing this project's custom water, foliage, fur and atmosphere passes. It remains a possible future choice, but it does not fix the current unshadowed mountain and grass materials by itself.

[Hillaire's atmosphere research](https://sebh.github.io/publications/egsr2020.pdf), published in 2020, describes a production atmosphere with scattering lookup tables and aerial perspective. The sky here uses wavelength-dependent extinction with Rayleigh and Henyey-Greenstein phase functions. It is an analytic optical-depth approximation. It does not implement the paper's full lookup-table or multiple-scattering solution.

Guerrilla's [Nubis³](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/nubis-cubed), published in 2023, explains why its newer system moved beyond layered clouds toward voxel volumes. Our new sky integrates procedural three-dimensional cloud density along 96 view samples on High, with six light samples per occupied point, height shaping, detail erosion, Beer extinction and directional scattering. It replaces the earlier two-dimensional cloud mask. It remains a bounded cloud layer with an approximate secondary scattering lobe, not Nubis³'s voxel authoring, temporal reconstruction or full cloud system.

Woodland air now integrates 48 shadow samples with forward scattering and moving density variation. Sunlight has more range relative to ambient fill, so tree shadows and warm highlights can describe shape. High retains ACES and uses a 64-entry HDR grading LUT, full-resolution opaque color, longer cascaded shadows, detailed terrain and SMAA High with 8× MSAA. TAA was evaluated and rejected for this pass: moving foliage, CPU-deformed hair and transparent water lack complete velocity buffers, and the first capture had poor thin-foliage coverage. Spatial antialiasing keeps those silhouettes stable. [Unity's camera reference](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.3/Documentation/Manual/urp/camera-component-reference.html) documents the compatible spatial methods and MSAA support.

A live 256-pixel HDR reflection probe follows the player and captures changing sky and scenery over individual faces. This adds specular environment lighting, not diffuse global illumination. Unity has [reported update issues with manually requested probe refreshes](https://issuetracker.unity.com/issues/4781/urp-realtime-reflection-probes-do-not-update-when-renderprobe-is-being-called-once-per-second); this implementation uses the engine's EveryFrame refresh mode and time slicing instead. The river captures show its environment fallback alongside screen-space reflections.

The quality ceiling takes priority for this pass. Validation still runs serially with CPU and memory limits and a 30 FPS presentation cap to avoid repeating the desktop lockup. Those execution limits are independent of the game's chosen shading and geometry detail.
